## 1. Introduction and Purpose

Meridian Petroleum Corporation (“the Company”) is committed to conducting its exploration, production, refining, and distribution operations to the highest standards of safety, integrity, and respect for its people. This Human Resources Policy Manual (“the Manual”) sets out the principles, standards, and procedures that govern employment across all Company sites, including corporate offices, onshore fields, offshore platforms, refineries, and terminals.

This Manual applies to all employees, contractors performing employee-equivalent roles, and temporary staff, unless a specific clause states otherwise. Employees are required to read, understand, and comply with this Manual as a condition of employment. It supplements, and is to be read together with, the Company's Health, Safety and Environment (HSE) Management System, Code of Business Conduct, and applicable local labour law.

### 1.1 Guiding Principles

- Safety of people and protection of the environment take priority over production and cost targets.
- Employment decisions are based on merit, competence, and business need, free from discrimination.
- Employees are treated with dignity, fairness, and respect at every stage of the employment relationship.
- The Company complies with all applicable labour, immigration, and health and safety legislation in every jurisdiction in which it operates.
- Where local law sets a higher standard than this Manual, local law prevails.

## 2. Scope, Governance and Policy Review

This Manual is issued and maintained by the Human Resources Department and approved by the Executive Committee. It applies Company-wide; country and site HR teams may issue local supplements to address jurisdiction-specific legal requirements, provided such supplements do not lower the standards set out here.

### 2.1 Review Cycle

This Manual is reviewed at least every two years, or sooner following a material change in law, a significant incident, or a merger, acquisition, or restructuring. All revisions are version-controlled and communicated to employees through the Company intranet and site inductions.

## 3. Recruitment, Selection and Onboarding

### 3.1 Recruitment Principles

Recruitment is conducted through fair, transparent, and competitive processes. Vacancies are filled based on qualifications, experience, competency assessments, and, where applicable, statutory local-content or nationalisation requirements. The Company does not charge recruitment fees to candidates.

### 3.2 Pre-Employment Screening

Given the safety-critical nature of oil and gas operations, offers of employment for operational, offshore, and site-based roles are conditional on successful completion of:

- Verification of identity, qualifications, and professional certifications.
- Reference checks covering the previous five years of employment, or as permitted by local law.
- A pre-employment medical examination, including assessment of fitness for offshore or confined-space work where relevant.
- A pre-employment drug and alcohol screen, in line with Section 9.
- Background and, where legally permissible, security screening for roles with access to sensitive sites or information.

### 3.3 Onboarding and Induction

All new starters complete a Company induction covering this Manual, the Code of Business Conduct, and general HSE requirements before their first day at any operational site. Site-specific inductions, including emergency response, permit-to-work systems, and platform or plant familiarisation, are mandatory before unsupervised access is granted and must be completed regardless of role or seniority.

### 3.4 Probation

New employees typically serve a probationary period of three to six months, as specified in their employment contract, during which performance and conduct are formally reviewed. The Company may extend probation once, by written notice, or confirm, extend, or end employment at the end of the period in accordance with the notice terms of the contract and applicable law.

## 4. Employment Classification and Working Hours

### 4.1 Categories of Employment

|                              |                                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Category**           | **Description**                                                                                                                               |
| Permanent                    | Ongoing employment with no fixed end date, subject to standard notice provisions.                                                                   |
| Fixed-Term                   | Employment for a defined project, campaign, or period, ending on the stated date or event.                                                          |
| Rotational (Offshore/Remote) | Employment on a defined work/rest rotation, e.g. 14/14, 28/28, per site roster.                                                                     |
| Contractor / Agency          | Engaged through a third-party contract; governed by the applicable service agreement and, where relevant, this Manual's HSE and conduct provisions. |

### 4.2 Standard Working Hours

Standard office and onshore working hours are 40 hours per week, typically Monday to Friday, exclusive of meal breaks, unless local law or site agreement specifies otherwise. Operational, shift, and offshore personnel work in accordance with approved rosters, which comply with statutory maximum working-hour and rest-period requirements, including the mandatory minimum 11 consecutive hours of rest in any 24-hour period for safety-critical roles, or the applicable local standard if higher.

### 4.3 Overtime

Overtime is authorised in advance by the relevant line manager and compensated in accordance with local law and the employee's contract, either as premium pay or compensatory time off. Fatigue management limits apply to safety-critical and offshore roles; supervisors must not schedule overtime that would breach mandatory rest requirements.

### 4.4 Offshore and Remote Site Rotations

- Rotational schedules, transport arrangements, and standby pay are defined in site-specific rosters issued by Operations and HR.
- Employees on rotation are entitled to designated rest days and are not required to perform Company work during off-rotation periods, except in a declared emergency.
- Helicopter and marine transport to offshore installations follows mandatory safety briefings, survival training (e.g. HUET certification), and weight/manifest procedures.

## 5. Compensation, Benefits and Allowances

### 5.1 Base Pay

Salaries are determined by role, grade, location, experience, and market benchmarking, and are reviewed periodically. Pay is disbursed monthly by bank transfer, with a payslip detailing gross pay, deductions, and net pay.

- Retirement / pension contributions in line with local statutory schemes and Company top-up where applicable.
- Annual leave, as set out in Section 6.
- Training, tuition assistance, and professional certification support relevant to the employee's role.

### 5.4 Performance and Reward

Eligible employees participate in an annual performance review linked to a discretionary bonus scheme. Bonus awards reflect individual performance, HSE record, and Company results; a serious safety violation, regardless of business performance, may reduce or eliminate an individual's award.

## 7. Health, Safety and Environment (HSE)

HSE is a core value of the Company, not merely a compliance obligation. Every employee, contractor, and visitor has the right and the duty to stop any activity they reasonably believe to be unsafe, without fear of reprisal. This right, commonly referred to as “Stop Work Authority,” applies at every level of the organisation.

### 7.1 Employee Safety Obligations

- Complete all mandatory HSE training and certifications before performing safety-critical tasks, and keep certifications current.
- Wear and maintain personal protective equipment (PPE) as specified for the task and location.
- Comply with permit-to-work, lock-out/tag-out, confined-space entry, and working-at-height procedures.
- Report all incidents, near misses, and hazardous conditions immediately through the Company's HSE reporting system, no matter how minor.
- Participate in emergency drills, musters, and evacuation exercises as scheduled.

### 7.2 Incident Reporting and Investigation

All injuries, spills, fires, equipment failures, and near-miss events must be reported to the site supervisor and HSE Department without delay, and in any case within the timeframe specified in the site HSE procedure. Serious incidents trigger a formal investigation; findings are used to improve procedures, not to assign blame, except where investigation reveals wilful misconduct or gross negligence, which is addressed under Section 12.

### 7.3 Fitness for Duty

Employees in safety-critical, offshore, or driving roles must maintain the medical fitness standard required for their position and disclose any change in health status that could affect their ability to work safely. Periodic medical reassessment is mandatory for offshore and safety-critical personnel.

### 7.4 Environmental Responsibility

Employees must comply with environmental permits, spill-prevention procedures, and waste-management protocols applicable to their site. Any actual or potential environmental release must be reported immediately in accordance with the site emergency response plan.

## 8. Code of Conduct and Workplace Behaviour

### 8.1 Standards of Conduct

Employees are expected to act honestly, professionally, and in the best interests of the Company at all times, including when representing the Company off-site or engaging with contractors, communities, and government officials.

### 8.2 Anti-Harassment and Anti-Discrimination

The Company prohibits harassment, bullying, and discrimination on the basis of race, gender, age, religion, disability, national origin, sexual orientation, marital status, or any other characteristic protected by law. This applies to conduct at Company premises, offshore installations, work-related travel, accommodation camps, and Company-sponsored events.

- Complaints may be raised confidentially with HR, a supervisor, or through the Whistleblower channel described in Section 11.
- All complaints are investigated promptly, impartially, and with appropriate confidentiality.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a good-faith concern is itself a disciplinary offence.

### 8.3 Anti-Bribery and Conflicts of Interest

Employees must not offer, give, solicit, or accept bribes, kickbacks, or improper payments, including facilitation payments to government officials, in connection with permits, customs clearance, licences, or contracts. Employees must declare, in writing to HR or Compliance, any actual or potential conflict of interest, including outside business interests, family relationships with contractors or vendors, or financial interests in competing businesses.

### 8.4 Use of Company Assets, IT and Data

Company equipment, systems, and data must be used responsibly and primarily for business purposes. Employees must comply with the Company's Information Security Policy, protect confidential and commercially sensitive information (including seismic, reserves, and production data), and report suspected cyber-security incidents immediately.

## 9. Drug, Alcohol and Fatigue Policy

Given the safety-critical nature of oil and gas operations, the Company operates a strict drug and alcohol policy across all sites, offshore installations, and Company vehicles.

- Employees must not be under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs, or misuse prescription medication, while on duty, on Company premises, offshore, or operating Company vehicles or equipment.
- Random, pre-employment, post-incident, and reasonable-suspicion testing may be conducted in accordance with local law and site procedure.
- Employees taking prescribed medication that may affect alertness or coordination must disclose this to their supervisor or site medic before undertaking safety-critical duties.
- A confirmed positive test, refusal to test, or tampering with a sample is treated as gross misconduct under Section 12.
- Employees who voluntarily seek help for a substance dependency issue before any policy breach will be supported through the Employee Assistance Programme, in confidence.

## 10. Performance Management and Training

### 10.1 Performance Reviews

All employees receive a formal performance review at least annually, with informal check-ins recommended throughout the year. Reviews assess delivery against objectives, competencies, and adherence to HSE and conduct standards, and inform development planning, promotion, and reward decisions.

### 10.2 Underperformance

Where performance falls below the required standard, the manager will discuss concerns with the employee, agree a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) with clear objectives and a defined review period, and provide appropriate support and training. Continued underperformance after a fair PIP process may lead to formal action under Section 12.

### 10.3 Training and Competency Assurance

The Company maintains a competency assurance framework for safety-critical roles; employees may not perform tasks for which they are not certified. Mandatory refresher training (e.g. first aid, firefighting, H2S awareness, survival training) must be completed before certification expiry, and site access may be suspended if certification lapses.

## 11. Grievance and Whistleblower Procedures

### 11.1 Grievance Procedure

Employees with a work-related concern are encouraged to raise it first with their line manager. If unresolved, or if the concern involves the line manager, the employee may escalate in writing to HR, who will acknowledge receipt within 5 working days and aim to resolve the matter within 20 working days, keeping the employee informed of progress.

### 11.2 Whistleblower / Speak-Up Channel

The Company operates a confidential Speak-Up channel, independent of line management, for reporting suspected violations of law, this Manual, the Code of Business Conduct, or safety standards. Reports may be made anonymously where permitted by local law. Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith, or against anyone who assists an investigation, is strictly prohibited and is itself a disciplinary offence.

## 12. Disciplinary Procedure

### 12.1 Principles

Disciplinary action is applied fairly, consistently, and proportionately, following investigation and with the employee given the opportunity to respond before any decision is made. Employees are entitled to be accompanied by a colleague or, where local law provides, a union representative at any formal disciplinary meeting.

### 12.2 Stages of Discipline

|                       |                                                                                     |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Stage**       | **Typical Outcome**                                                           |
| Verbal Warning        | Recorded on file; addresses first minor infractions.                                |
| Written Warning       | Formal written notice; sets expectations and a review period.                       |
| Final Written Warning | Issued for repeated or more serious misconduct; explicit warning of dismissal risk. |
| Dismissal             | For gross misconduct, or persistent misconduct following prior warnings.            |

### 12.3 Gross Misconduct

The following are treated as gross misconduct and may result in summary dismissal, without progressing through prior stages, subject to investigation and applicable law:

- Serious or wilful breach of a safety procedure that endangers life, the environment, or Company assets, including bypassing safety-critical equipment or falsifying safety checks.
- Being under the influence of drugs or alcohol on duty, or a confirmed positive test as described in Section 9.
- Theft, fraud, bribery, or falsification of Company records, timesheets, or expense claims.
- Serious harassment, discrimination, violence, or threats of violence.
- Deliberate disclosure of confidential, commercially sensitive, or proprietary data without authorisation.
- Serious insubordination or wilful refusal to follow a lawful and reasonable instruction.

## 13. Termination of Employment

### 13.1 Notice Periods

Notice periods are as specified in the employee's contract of employment and applicable local law, and generally increase with length of service and seniority. Either party may terminate by providing the required notice, or pay in lieu of notice at the Company's discretion, except in cases of gross misconduct.

### 13.2 Resignation

Employees wishing to resign should submit written notice to their line manager and HR. A full and final settlement, including any accrued but untaken leave, is processed in the final payroll cycle in accordance with local law.

### 13.3 Redundancy

Where operational requirements, restructuring, or field decommissioning necessitate workforce reduction, the Company follows a fair, transparent, and legally compliant redundancy process, including consultation, objective selection criteria, and statutory or contractual severance payments.

### 13.4 Exit Process

- Return of all Company property, including PPE, IT equipment, access cards, and offshore survival kit.
- Revocation of site access, IT system access, and any Company-issued certifications tied to employment.
- Exit interview offered to gather feedback and confirm final entitlements.

## 14. Data Protection and Employee Records

The Company collects and processes personal data, including medical fitness records required for offshore and safety-critical roles, in accordance with applicable data protection law and its Privacy Policy. Access to personnel files is restricted to authorised HR personnel and is disclosed to third parties only where legally required or with the employee's consent. Employees may request access to their personal file through HR, subject to applicable legal exceptions.
